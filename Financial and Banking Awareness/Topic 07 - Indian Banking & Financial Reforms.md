# Topic 7: Indian Banking & Financial Reforms — Detailed Conceptual Bank

This topic traces the journey of Indian banking from a heavily regulated system to a liberalized, technology-driven one. For the IBPS RRB IT Officer exam, the focus is often on the Narasimham Committees, the Nationalization phases, and the IT-driven reforms (like CBS and INFINET) that enabled modern banking.
## 📝 Quick Revision Cheat Sheet (Before you start)

- **Phase of Nationalization:** 14 major banks were nationalized in 1969, followed by 6 more in 1980 .
- **Narasimham Committee I (1991):** The cornerstone of reforms. It recommended reducing the SLR and CRR, introducing prudential norms (Capital Adequacy, NPA recognition), and allowing entry of private banks .
- **Narasimham Committee II (1998):** Focused on strengthening the system further. It recommended mergers of strong banks, setting up Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs) to deal with NPAs, and raising the capital adequacy ratio .
- **Technology Reforms:** Key outcomes included the establishment of INFINET (communication backbone), RTGS (Real-Time Gross Settlement), and the rollout of Core Banking Solutions (CBS) to make the bank a "single entity" .
- **RRB Structure & Evolution:** Established in 1975 (RRB Act 1976) with a shareholding of Central Govt (50%), State Govt (15%), and Sponsor Bank (35%) . Recent reforms have consolidated them into "One State-One RRB" to improve viability .
